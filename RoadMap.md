# Roadmap: Zero → AI Master's in France → EU Passport

**Starting point:** 3rd year BSc (distance mode), real academic level ~Class 12, Arch Linux + git/dotfiles experience (real asset), zero coding/ML background otherwise.

---

## Phase 0 — Admin Reality Check (do this early, costs nothing)

- Email **Campus France India** and ask specifically how your university's distance-mode BSc is evaluated for Master's admission. The France-India mutual recognition agreement covers UGC-recognized institutions in general, but selective programs still judge your actual transcript and rigor — better to know now than at application time.
- Because a distance degree can be viewed as less rigorous than a full-time one, treat your **GitHub, Kaggle, and project portfolio as mandatory**, not optional — it's what offsets that gap.

---

## Phase 1 — Math & Physics Foundations
*(NCERT Class 11–12, run alongside your current BSc coursework)*

**Math, in this order:**
1. Sets, Relations & Functions
2. Limits and Derivatives (first real calculus exposure)
3. Sequences and Series
4. Statistics & Probability
5. **Matrices and Determinants** ← basically linear algebra in disguise
6. Continuity, Differentiability & Application of Derivatives (optimization intuition starts here)
7. **Vector Algebra**
8. Integrals & Application of Integrals
9. **Probability** (12th level — Bayes' theorem)
- Lower priority for AI purposes (but still needed for marks): Trig, Straight Lines/Conics, Complex Numbers, Linear Programming, 3D Geometry

**Physics, in this order:**
1. Units and Measurements
2. Motion in a Straight Line / Motion in a Plane
3. Laws of Motion, Work-Energy-Power
4. Electrostatics + Current Electricity (heavily weighted)
5. Magnetism (Moving Charges, EM Induction)
6. Semiconductor Electronics, Dual Nature of Radiation, Atoms & Nuclei
- Lower priority: Optics (still needed for exam, not for AI)

> Physics is here to keep your degree on track — math gets first claim on your time.

---

## Phase 2 — Python & Coding Fundamentals
*(Start immediately, runs in parallel with Phase 1 — needs no math to begin)*

1. **Python syntax**: variables, loops, conditionals, functions
2. **Data structures**: lists, dicts, sets, tuples
3. File handling, basic error handling (try/except)
4. Basic OOP (classes/objects) — enough to read others' code
5. **Core CS thinking** (parallel): Big-O intuition, arrays/stacks/queues/hash maps, recursion, sorting/searching
6. Write small scripts, commit each to git — start a real learning-log repo alongside your dotfiles

---

## Phase 3 — Data Handling Stack
*(Once Phase 2 basics feel comfortable)*

- **NumPy** — array ops; your Matrices/Vectors chapters become literal code here
- **Pandas** — loading, cleaning, filtering real data (most of actual ML work)
- **Matplotlib / Seaborn** — visualization
- Practice on real messy datasets (Kaggle), not toy examples

---

## Phase 4 — Classical Machine Learning
*(Only once Phase 1's Probability + Matrices are genuinely solid — don't skip ahead)*

- **scikit-learn**: linear/logistic regression, decision trees, k-means, k-NN
- Core concepts: train/test split, overfitting vs underfitting, cross-validation, evaluation metrics (accuracy, precision/recall, confusion matrix)
- Course: Andrew Ng's Machine Learning (Coursera, free to audit)

---

## Phase 5 — University-Level Math
*(Upgrade the 11th/12th versions to the real thing)*

- **Linear Algebra proper**: vector spaces, eigenvalues/eigenvectors, SVD, orthogonality
- **Multivariable Calculus + Optimization**: gradients, Jacobians, Hessians, gradient descent, Lagrange multipliers (this *is* backpropagation)
- **Probability & Statistics, rigorous**: random variables, distributions, MLE, Bayesian inference, hypothesis testing
- **Discrete Math**: logic, combinatorics, graph theory (shows up in decision trees, graph neural nets)
- Lower priority unless your program needs it: Differential Equations

---

## Phase 6 — CS / Software Engineering Depth

- **Data Structures & Algorithms** (proper): trees, graphs, dynamic programming
- **Statistical Learning Theory**: bias-variance tradeoff, regularization, why models generalize
- **Databases**: SQL at minimum — real ML work is mostly data wrangling
- **Software engineering practices**: testing, git branches/PRs, reusable code (not just notebooks) — distance programs usually skip this, practice deliberately
- **LaTeX** — French Master's reports/thesis expect it; learn early, not in a scramble

---

## Phase 7 — Deep Learning & Modern AI
*(Once classical ML and the math upgrade make sense)*

- Neural network basics: neurons/layers, forward pass, backprop (this is Phase 5's calculus, applied)
- **PyTorch** (standard in research and most AI Master's programs, including in France)
- CNNs (images) → RNNs/Transformers (sequences, language)
- Course: fast.ai (practice-first approach, good alongside parallel theory)
- Then: Attention/Transformers architecture, Hugging Face ecosystem (using + fine-tuning pretrained models)
- Read a few key papers even before you follow all the math — builds field fluency

---

## Phase 8 — Portfolio (ongoing throughout, not a separate phase)

- 2–3 solid, well-documented GitHub projects (good README matters)
- Kaggle competitions — verifiable signal of ability
- Dated learning log committed to git — visible proof of self-directed work, useful given the distance-degree question in Phase 0

---

## Phase 9 — English Proficiency

- IELTS or TOEFL — needed since the target AI Master's (e.g. Grenoble/Ensimag) is English-taught
- Start once your academic basics are steady; don't leave it to the final year

---

## Phase 10 — Applying to France

- Apply via the **Etudes en France (EEF)** portal (mandatory route for Indian students)
- Application windows vary by track — roughly October to January/March depending on program type; check your specific program's exact deadline, don't rely on a single fixed date
- A 3-year Bachelor's is sufficient for M1 entry to Grenoble's AI Master's (Ensimag/UGA) — confirmed by their published admission criteria
- French B2 is **not** required for this specific English-taught track — don't over-invest in French yet

---

## Phase 11 — During the Master's in France

- Confirm with your specific program when Alternance (work-study) contracts open up — varies by program (some allow M1, some only M2), don't assume "after one semester" applies to yours
- Non-EU students on a student residence permit generally don't need separate work authorization to sign an apprenticeship contract, but confirm current rules at enrollment

---

## Phase 12 — After the Master's: Building Residency

- Convert Alternance → permanent **CDI** job if possible — this is what builds the "stable, anchored" residency history French prefectures look for
- Budget roughly **2 more years of stable French employment after graduation** before applying for naturalization — the reduced 2-year window is not simply "the day you graduate"

---

## Phase 13 — French for Citizenship
*(Low priority now, becomes real ~mid-Master's or after)*

- Naturalization now requires **B2 French** (oral + written) as of Jan 1, 2026 — this is separate from your Master's language requirement
- Casual/gradual study is fine early on; get serious as you approach the application window

---

## Phase 14 — Naturalization / Passport

- Standard requirement: 5 years residence; reduced to **2 years** for holders of a French Master's-level diploma or higher (Article 21-18, Code civil)
- New 2026 requirements: **B2 French** + a **40-question civic exam** on French history/institutions
- Naturalization is **discretionary** — meeting criteria doesn't guarantee approval; recent policy scrutinizes applicants whose income comes mostly from abroad, reinforcing the value of stable local employment
- Filing-to-decree processing: typically **18–24 months**
- **Note:** France generally allows dual nationality, but India does not — becoming a French citizen would mean renouncing Indian citizenship (an OCI card is available afterward for many practical rights in India, but not citizenship itself)

---

## Suggested Order of Attack (right now)

1. **Math + Python, in parallel, starting today** (Phases 1 & 2)
2. Physics, at "solid pass" effort, per your actual syllabus
3. Move into Phase 3 (NumPy/Pandas) once Python basics feel natural
4. Only start Phase 4 (classical ML) once Probability + Matrices are genuinely solid
5. English test prep, whenever basics are steady — don't leave to the last year
6. Everything from Phase 5 onward (university-level math, DL, portfolio) ramps up as you approach application time
7. French language and the citizenship-specific steps (Phases 13–14) can wait — they only become relevant once you're actually in France
